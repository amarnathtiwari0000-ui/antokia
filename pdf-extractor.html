Extract Pages from a PDF Online for Free

Antokia's PDF page extractor lets you pull only the pages you need out of a large PDF and save them as a new, smaller file. It is useful when a form asks for just a few pages, when you want to share one chapter of a report, or when you need to split a long scan into parts.

How to Extract Pages from a PDF

  1. Upload your PDF file.
  2. Click page numbers, or type pages like 1,4,7-9 in the box and press Apply.
  3. Check the Selected count.
  4. Click Extract Pages and Download to get the new PDF.

When Is This Useful?

  1. Uploading only the required pages to a job, exam or bank portal with a size limit.
  2. Sharing one section of a long document without sending everything.
  3. Splitting a large scanned file into smaller PDFs.
  4. Saving a single page, such as an ID or certificate, from a bundle of scans.

Extract vs Remove Pages

Extracting keeps the pages you select. Removing deletes the pages you select and keeps the rest. If you only want to drop a few pages from a long file, use Remove PDF Pages. If you want to keep only a few pages, extracting is faster.

Tips for Best Results

  1. Page numbers here start from 1 for the first page of the file, which may differ from the number printed on the paper.
  2. Selected pages are saved in their original order.
  3. If the new file is still large, reduce it with Compress PDF.
  4. To combine extracted parts again, use Merge PDF.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I extract pages from a PDF?

Upload your PDF, select the pages you want or type a range such as 2-5, and click Extract Pages and Download. The selected pages are saved as a new PDF.

Is the PDF page extractor free?

Yes. It is free to use and needs no account.

Can I extract non-continuous pages?

Yes. You can enter pages such as 1,4,7-9 and they will be saved in page order.

What is the difference between extracting and removing pages?

Extracting keeps only the pages you choose. Removing deletes the pages you choose and keeps the rest.

Are my PDF files uploaded to a server?

No. Processing is done in your browser, so the file stays on your device.
