Successive Discounts Explained: Why 20% + 10% Is Not 30%

Two discounts together are not the same as one big discount. Learn the right way to calculate the real saving.

Updated: November 2026 · Antokia Guide

In a sale you often see offers like "20% off plus extra 10% off". Many people add them and think the total is 30%, but that is not how it works. The second discount is taken on the already reduced price, so the real discount is a little less. Here is how to calculate it correctly.

Formula: Final price = Original price x (1 - d1/100) x (1 - d2/100). Total discount % = 100 - (final price / original price x 100).

Example: 20% and 10% on Rs 2,000

Step 1: First discount

20% of Rs 2,000 is Rs 400, so the price becomes Rs 1,600.

Step 2: Second discount

10% of Rs 1,600 is Rs 160, so the price becomes Rs 1,440.

Step 3: Total discount

Total saving is Rs 560, which is 28% of Rs 2,000, not 30%.

If it were a single 30% discount, you would pay Rs 1,400. So 20% plus 10% successive is slightly worse than a flat 30%.

Flat Amount Off vs Percentage Off

To compare a flat amount with a percentage, convert both to the final price. For a Rs 3,000 item, "Rs 500 off" gives Rs 2,500. "15% off" gives Rs 2,550. So here the flat discount is better. On a Rs 6,000 item, 15% gives Rs 5,100, while Rs 500 off gives Rs 5,500, so the percentage is better.

Where GST Fits

Tips While Shopping

  • Always compare the final price you pay, not the percentage on the tag.
  • Check delivery, packaging and convenience fees.
  • Compare the price on two or three sites before buying.
  • Be careful with offers that raise the price first and then show a discount.
