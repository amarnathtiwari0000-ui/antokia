Resume for Freshers: Format, Sections and Examples

No work experience yet? Here is how freshers can build a clear one-page resume that gets noticed.

Updated: November 2026 · Antokia Guide

A fresher does not have a long job history, but a good resume can still make a strong first impression. Recruiters look at a resume for only a few seconds, so it must be clear, short and easy to scan. This guide covers the sections, order and common mistakes for fresh graduates.

The Basic Structure

SectionWhat to writeLength
HeaderName, phone, professional email, city, LinkedIn or portfolio link2 to 3 lines
Summary or objectiveWho you are and what role you want, with 1 or 2 skills2 to 3 lines
EducationDegree, college, year, marks or CGPALatest first
SkillsTools, languages and software you really knowShort list
ProjectsWhat you built, tools used, and the result2 to 4 projects
Internships or experienceRole, work done, measurable resultIf any
Achievements and certificatesAwards, courses, competitionsShort list

Writing Tips for Freshers

  • Keep it to one page. A fresher resume rarely needs more.
  • Use action words: built, designed, analysed, led, organised.
  • Add numbers: "Built a website used by 200 students" is stronger than "worked on a website".
  • Match the job: change the skills and summary for each application.
  • Use a simple font and layout so it is easy to read on screen and in print.
  • Be honest: list only skills you can explain in an interview.

Project Example

Weak: Made a calculator project.
Better: Built a loan calculator web app using HTML, CSS and JavaScript that shows EMI, total interest and a payment chart; tested with 20 users.

Common Mistakes

  • Spelling and grammar errors.
  • An unprofessional email address.
  • A photo, date of birth or personal details that the role does not need.
  • Long paragraphs instead of short points.
  • Copying a template with fake or exaggerated claims.
