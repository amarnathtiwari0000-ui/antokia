Loan EMI Guide

How to Calculate EMI for a Loan

Learn how monthly loan EMI is calculated, understand the EMI formula and see a simple example using loan amount, interest rate and tenure.

Easy Guide Loan EMI Antokia.com

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the amount a borrower generally pays every month toward a loan. An EMI usually includes both the principal amount and the interest charged on the outstanding loan balance.

Whether you are checking a home loan, personal loan, car loan or another fixed-rate loan, understanding EMI can help you estimate your monthly repayment before applying for a loan.

What Is EMI?

EMI is a fixed periodic payment used to repay a loan over a specified period. The exact amount depends mainly on the loan principal, interest rate and loan tenure.

In simple words:

A higher loan amount or interest rate generally increases the EMI, while a longer repayment period generally reduces the monthly EMI but can increase the total interest paid.

EMI Calculation Formula

The commonly used mathematical formula for calculating EMI is:

EMI Formula
EMI = [P × R × (1 + R)N] ÷ [(1 + R)N − 1]

P = Principal loan amount

R = Monthly interest rate

N = Number of monthly instalments

How to Calculate EMI Step by Step

  1. Enter the total loan amount.
  2. Convert the annual interest rate into a monthly rate.
  3. Convert the loan tenure from years into the total number of monthly instalments.
  4. Apply the EMI formula.
  5. The result gives the approximate monthly EMI for the entered loan details.

The exact amount may vary slightly depending on the calculation method and lender terms.

Factors That Affect Your EMI

  • Loan Amount: A larger principal generally results in a higher EMI.
  • Interest Rate: A higher interest rate generally increases the EMI and total interest cost.
  • Loan Tenure: A longer tenure generally reduces the monthly EMI but may increase total interest paid over the loan period.

EMI vs Total Interest

The monthly EMI is not the same as the total cost of borrowing. Over the complete loan tenure, the borrower may pay more than the original principal because of interest.

Therefore, while comparing loan options, it can be useful to look at both the monthly EMI and the total interest payable, rather than considering the EMI alone.
