What is SIP?

SIP stands for Systematic Investment Plan. It is a method of investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, commonly every month, into a mutual fund scheme.

Instead of investing one large amount at once, an investor can contribute smaller amounts regularly. The future value of a SIP depends on the investment amount, duration and the return assumed for the calculation.

How to Calculate SIP Returns?

A commonly used SIP future-value formula can be used to estimate the value of regular monthly investments.

M = P × [((1 + i)n − 1) ÷ i] × (1 + i)

This is a commonly used monthly SIP estimation formula.

Meaning of the Formula

  • M = Estimated maturity or future value
  • P = Monthly SIP investment
  • i = Monthly rate of return
  • n = Total number of monthly investments
How to convert annual return into monthly rate:

If the assumed annual return is 12%, a simple monthly-rate calculation uses 12% ÷ 12 = 1% per month. Different calculators may use different return conventions, so results can vary slightly.

Example: ₹5,000 Monthly SIP for 15 Years

Suppose you invest ₹5,000 every month for 15 years and assume an annual return of 12% for illustration.

Particular Example
Monthly SIP ₹5,000
Investment Period 15 Years
Assumed Annual Return 12%
Total Amount Invested ₹9,00,000
Estimated Future Value Approx. ₹25 Lakh

This example is an estimate based on an assumed 12% annual return. Actual mutual fund returns can be higher or lower and are not guaranteed.

How to Calculate SIP Step-by-Step

  1. Decide your monthly SIP investment amount.
  2. Select the investment period in years.
  3. Enter an assumed annual return for estimation.
  4. Convert the annual rate into the monthly rate where required.
  5. Apply the SIP formula or use an online calculator.
  6. Compare your total investment with the estimated future value.

Factors That Affect SIP Returns

  • Monthly investment: A higher monthly contribution can increase the potential future value.
  • Investment duration: A longer investment period gives more time for compounding.
  • Rate of return: A higher assumed return produces a higher estimated future value.
  • Market performance: Actual mutual fund returns fluctuate with market conditions.
  • Investment timing: The timing of investments can affect actual results.
Important:

SIP calculators provide estimates based on an assumed rate of return. Mutual fund investments are market-linked and actual returns are not guaranteed. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
