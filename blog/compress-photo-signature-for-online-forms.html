How to Compress Photo and Signature to 20KB or 50KB for Online Forms

Getting a "file size too large" error on an exam, job or admission form? This guide shows how to reduce your photo and signature to the required KB size without making them blurry.

Updated: October 2026 · Antokia Guide

Many online forms, such as government job applications, entrance exams and college admissions, accept photos and signatures only below a strict file size like 20KB, 50KB or 100KB. A photo taken with a phone is often several MB, so it gets rejected.

Quick answer: Resize the image to the dimensions your form asks for, then compress it and lower the quality slider until the file is under the limit. Both steps can be done free on Antokia, and the image never leaves your browser.

Step-by-Step: Reduce Photo Size in KB

Step 1: Read the form rules

Note the maximum size (for example 50KB), the dimensions (for example 200 x 230 pixels) and the allowed format (JPG or PNG). Rules differ for every form, so always follow the official notification.

Step 2: Resize the image

Open the Image Resizer, upload your photo and enter the required width and height. Smaller dimensions are the biggest help in reducing size.

Step 3: Compress the image

Open the Image Compressor, upload the resized photo and reduce the quality slider. The tool shows the original and compressed size, so you can stop once it is under the limit.

Step 4: Check and upload

Confirm the final size, format and that your face or signature is still clearly visible, then upload it to the form.

How to Make a Good Signature Image

  • Sign with a black or blue pen on plain white paper.
  • Take the photo in good light, without shadows.
  • Crop closely around the signature so there is little empty space.
  • Resize, then compress to the required KB.
Tip: If the file is still too big, reduce the dimensions further instead of dropping quality too low. This keeps the image sharper.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Compressing without resizing, so large dimensions keep the file big.
  • Compressing an already compressed image again and again, which reduces quality.
  • Using the wrong format, such as PNG when the form asks for JPG.
  • Ignoring the dimension rules and checking only the KB size.
