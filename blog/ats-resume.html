What Is an ATS Resume? How to Make an ATS-Friendly Resume

Learn how Applicant Tracking Systems read resumes and how you can create a simple, professional and ATS-friendly resume.

When applying for a job online, your resume may not always be reviewed by a recruiter first. Many companies use software called an Applicant Tracking System (ATS) to collect, organize and filter job applications.

This is why having an ATS-friendly resume is important. A well-structured resume can make it easier for the system to read your information and identify relevant skills and experience.

What Is an ATS?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. It is software used by many employers and recruitment teams to manage job applications.

An ATS can help companies collect resumes, organize applications and search for candidates based on information such as job titles, skills, qualifications and experience.

Because resumes can be processed electronically, using a clear and readable format is important.

What Is an ATS Resume?

An ATS resume is a resume designed with a simple and readable structure that can be easily processed by Applicant Tracking System software.

The goal is not to create a complicated design. Instead, the resume should clearly communicate your professional experience, skills, education and qualifications.

Why Is an ATS-Friendly Resume Important?

A professionally designed resume should work for both software and human readers. If important information is difficult to read or identify, it may reduce the effectiveness of your application.

  • Clear sections make information easier to identify.
  • Relevant keywords can help match your resume with a job description.
  • Simple formatting reduces parsing problems.
  • Standard headings make your experience easier to understand.
  • A clean layout also makes the resume easier for recruiters to review.

How to Make an ATS-Friendly Resume

1. Use a Simple Layout

Avoid making your resume unnecessarily complicated. Use a clean layout with clearly separated sections.

Important information such as your name, contact details, experience, education and skills should be easy to locate.

2. Use Standard Resume Headings

Use familiar section headings such as:

  • Professional Summary
  • Work Experience
  • Skills
  • Education
  • Certifications
  • Projects

3. Add Relevant Keywords

Read the job description carefully and identify the skills, qualifications and job-related terms that are relevant to your experience.

For example, if a job description mentions project management, safety management, risk assessment or compliance, include relevant terms naturally when they accurately describe your experience.

Do not add keywords that do not match your actual skills or experience.

4. Keep Your Job Experience Clear

For every position, clearly mention your job title, company, employment period and important responsibilities or achievements.

Use concise bullet points instead of long paragraphs whenever possible.

5. Use Readable Fonts

Choose a professional and easy-to-read font. Avoid decorative fonts that may make your resume difficult to read.

6. Avoid Unnecessary Graphics

Charts, decorative graphics, excessive icons and complicated layouts may make information harder to process.

For an ATS-focused resume, keep the design professional and simple.

7. Check Your Resume Before Applying

Before submitting your resume, check your spelling, dates, job titles, contact information and formatting.

Also make sure the skills mentioned in your resume are relevant to the position you are applying for.

Common ATS Resume Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using a highly complicated design.
  • Adding irrelevant keywords.
  • Using unclear section headings.
  • Writing very long paragraphs.
  • Leaving important skills out of the resume.
  • Using spelling mistakes or inconsistent formatting.
  • Adding false information just to match a job description.

What Should an ATS Resume Include?

A good ATS-friendly resume can include the following sections:

  • Full Name
  • Contact Information
  • Professional Summary
  • Work Experience
  • Key Skills
  • Education
  • Certifications
  • Projects or Achievements
