Why Your PDF to Word File Looks Wrong (and How to Fix It)

PDF to Word gave you only text or a messy layout? Here are the real reasons and simple fixes.

Updated: November 2026 · Antokia Guide

You convert a PDF to Word and the result is wrong: only plain text, missing images, broken tables, or even a blank page. This is very common, and it is not always the tool at fault. It depends on how the PDF was made. Here is why it happens and what you can do.

Reason 1: The PDF Is a Scan or a Photo

If your PDF was made by scanning paper or saving photos, every page is just a picture. There is no real text inside it. A normal converter cannot read the letters, so Word gets a picture or nothing. To get editable text from such a file you need OCR (optical character recognition), which reads text from images. Antokia PDF to Word does not do OCR, so for scanned documents use the Exact look mode to keep the page appearance, or use an OCR tool.

Reason 2: Complex Layout

PDFs store the position of each piece of text, not the idea of paragraphs and tables. Columns, text boxes, headers, footers and tables with merged cells are hard to rebuild in Word, so some manual clean-up may be needed.

Reason 3: Special Fonts

If the PDF uses a font that is not on your computer, Word replaces it, and spacing may shift. Install the font or choose a similar one after conversion.

How to Get the Best Result

Step 1: Check if the PDF has selectable text

Open the PDF and try to select a word with the mouse. If you can, it is text-based and should convert well.

Step 2: Choose the right mode

Use Editable for text PDFs. Use Exact look for scans.

Step 3: Convert and review

Open the Word file and check headings, tables, images and page breaks.

Step 4: Fix small issues

Adjust fonts, spacing and table borders in Word. It is faster than retyping the whole document.

Common Mistakes

  • Trying to edit a scanned PDF as if it had real text.
  • Not checking tables after conversion.
  • Converting a protected PDF without removing the password first.
  • Expecting a perfect copy for a very complex design.
