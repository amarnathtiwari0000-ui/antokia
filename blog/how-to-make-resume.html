A resume is a concise document that presents your education, experience, skills and relevant achievements to a potential employer. A well-organized resume makes it easier for a recruiter to understand your background and identify information relevant to a job. This guide explains how to create a simple and professional resume, what sections to include, how to format it and which common mistakes to avoid.

What Is a Resume?

A resume is a short summary of your professional background. It normally highlights information such as education, employment history, skills, projects, certifications and relevant achievements.

The purpose of a resume is not to describe every detail of your career. Instead, it should present the most relevant information clearly so that an employer can quickly understand your qualifications.

Important:

A resume should be customized for the type of job you are applying for. Relevant skills and experience should receive more attention than unrelated information.

What Should a Professional Resume Include?

The exact sections can vary depending on your experience, industry and the job, but many resumes contain the following:

👤

1. Name & Contact Information

Include your name, phone number, professional email address and relevant location or professional profile links.

📝

2. Professional Summary

Write a short summary of your experience, key skills and career direction.

🎓

3. Education

Mention relevant qualifications, institutions and completion dates.

💼

4. Work Experience

List relevant roles, employers, dates and responsibilities or achievements.

🛠️

5. Skills

Include skills that are relevant to the position and that you can demonstrate.

🏆

6. Additional Information

Certifications, projects, awards, languages or other relevant information can be included when useful.

How to Make a Resume Step by Step

Creating a resume becomes easier when the information is added in a logical order.

1

Add Your Contact Details

Enter your full name, phone number and a professional email address. Add relevant professional links if needed.

2

Write a Short Summary

Briefly describe your professional background, important skills and the type of role you are targeting.

3

Add Education

List relevant qualifications, institutions and dates in a consistent format.

4

Add Experience

Mention relevant jobs, internships, responsibilities, projects and measurable achievements where applicable.

5

Add Relevant Skills

Choose skills that match the position and accurately represent your abilities.

6

Review Before Sending

Check spelling, dates, contact information, formatting and file readability.

How to Write a Good Professional Summary

A professional summary should quickly explain who you are, what experience or skills you have and what type of opportunity you are seeking.

Example for an Experienced Candidate

"Safety professional with experience in construction and facility environments, with a focus on workplace safety, risk assessment, compliance and team coordination."

Example for a Fresher

"Recent graduate with an interest in business operations and digital tools, seeking an entry-level opportunity to apply analytical, communication and organizational skills."

These are examples only. Your summary should accurately describe your own background and should be adjusted to the job.

How to Write Work Experience on a Resume

Instead of only listing job titles, describe relevant responsibilities and achievements in a concise way.

Less Informative More Useful
Worked as Safety Officer Conducted workplace inspections, supported risk assessments and coordinated safety training activities.
Handled reports Prepared and maintained safety records, inspection reports and compliance follow-ups.

Use action-oriented descriptions where appropriate, but do not add responsibilities or achievements that you did not actually perform.

What Is an ATS-Friendly Resume?

ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. Some organizations use software to help manage and organize job applications.

An ATS-friendly resume generally uses straightforward formatting, standard section headings and readable text. Different ATS platforms process documents differently, so there is no single format that guarantees a particular result.

Useful ATS Practices:
  • Use clear section headings.
  • Use readable fonts and consistent formatting.
  • Include relevant job-related keywords naturally.
  • Avoid unnecessary graphics or complicated layouts.
  • Review the final PDF or document before uploading.

Should You Use a Simple Resume Design?

A simple resume design can make important information easier to find. This is especially useful when the resume contains several sections such as experience, education and skills.

However, the appropriate design depends on the industry and role. Creative positions may allow more visual flexibility, while traditional business, technical or administrative roles may benefit from straightforward formatting.

How Long Should a Resume Be?

There is no universal page limit for every profession.

For someone with limited experience, a concise one-page resume may be sufficient. Candidates with several years of relevant experience may need additional space to present important information.

Better Rule:

Focus on relevance rather than a fixed page count. Remove unnecessary information while keeping important qualifications that help explain your suitability for the role.

5 Common Resume Mistakes to Avoid

1. Incorrect Information

Never add false qualifications, experience, certifications or achievements.

2. Spelling & Grammar Errors

Typos can make a resume harder to read. Review the document carefully.

3. Unprofessional Email

Use a simple email address that is appropriate for professional communication.

4. Too Much Unrelated Information

Focus on information that is relevant to the role you are applying for.

5. Not Checking the Final File

Open the final PDF or document before sending it. Check page breaks, formatting, contact details and readability.

Should You Add a Photo to Your Resume?

Photo requirements differ by country, industry, employer and job application. Some applications may request a photo while others may not.

If the job application does not ask for a photograph, check the expectations for your location and industry before deciding whether to include one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I make a professional resume?
Start with your contact details, professional summary, education, relevant experience and skills. Use clear formatting and review the final document carefully.
What information should be included in a resume?
Common sections include contact information, professional summary, education, work experience, skills and relevant certifications, projects or achievements.
Should a resume be one page?
Not necessarily. The appropriate length depends on your experience, industry and the amount of relevant information.
Should I put a photo on my resume?
Photo requirements vary by country, employer and job. Include one when it is appropriate or specifically requested.
What is an ATS-friendly resume?
An ATS-friendly resume generally uses clear headings, straightforward formatting and readable text. Different applicant tracking systems may process documents differently.
Can I create a resume online?
Yes. Online resume makers can help organize contact details, education, experience and skills into a structured resume.

Conclusion

A professional resume does not need complicated design. Its main purpose is to present accurate, relevant and easy-to-read information about your education, experience and skills.

Choose a format that suits the job, keep the content relevant, check every detail and review the final document before sending it to an employer.
