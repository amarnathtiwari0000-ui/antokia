What Is a TIN Number? Meaning, Format and How to Find It in India

TIN, PAN, GSTIN, TAN... the tax numbers are confusing. Here is what TIN really means in India and which number you need.

Updated: November 2026 · Antokia Guide

You may have seen the term TIN on a tax form, a bank KYC form or an old business invoice and wondered what it is. TIN stands for Taxpayer Identification Number. The meaning depends on where it is used, so this guide explains the different cases in simple words.

Quick answer: In India, TIN was the 11-digit Taxpayer Identification Number given to businesses under VAT and sales tax. After GST started in July 2017, businesses use GSTIN instead. For individuals, your PAN works as your tax identification number.

What Is a TIN Number?

A TIN is a unique number used to identify a person or business for tax purposes. Different countries use different formats. In the USA it is the SSN or EIN, in the UK it is the UTR, and in India the PAN is used for most people.

TIN Number in India: The Three Meanings

Foreign forms (FATCA, CRS, W-8BEN)Your tax identification number in your country of residenceFor Indian residents this is the PAN
TIN-NSDLTax Information Network, the system that handles PAN and TDS workNot a personal number, it is the name of the system

Old VAT TIN Format

The old TIN had 11 digits. The first two digits showed the state code, and the rest identified the dealer. For example, a TIN starting with 07 belonged to Delhi. Since GST replaced VAT, new registrations get a GSTIN instead.

TIN vs GSTIN vs PAN

NumberLengthUsed for
PAN10 charactersIncome tax, bank and investment KYC for people and companies
GSTIN15 charactersGST registration of a business
Old TIN11 digitsVAT and sales tax before GST
TAN10 charactersDeducting or collecting tax at source (TDS and TCS)

A GSTIN already contains the PAN of the business inside it (characters 3 to 12), which is why the two are linked.

How to Find Your Tax Identification Number

For a person

Use your PAN. It is printed on your PAN card and on your income tax returns.

For a business registered under GST

Use your GSTIN, shown on your GST registration certificate and on the GST portal after you log in.

For an old VAT business

Check old VAT returns or the registration certificate. You may still need it for old records.

For a foreign form that asks for TIN

Write your PAN in the TIN field if the form asks for your Indian tax number.

Official Sites to Verify Details

For anything related to your tax number, use the official government portals only: the Income Tax e-filing portal for PAN related services and the GST portal for GSTIN search and registration. Never share your number or OTP with unknown websites or callers.

Common Mistakes

  • Searching for a TIN for a new business when you actually need a GSTIN.
  • Typing the PAN with a wrong letter or digit on a foreign tax form.
  • Sharing tax numbers on unofficial websites that ask for payment.
  • Assuming old VAT TIN records are no longer needed. Keep them for audits.
